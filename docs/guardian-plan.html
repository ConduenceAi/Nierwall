The demo, in five beats

  1. Ask out loud. ⌘⇧Space, “How's my Rhea loan?” A spoken answer and a live card, with a badge showing that a verified TEE model on NEAR AI Cloud wrote it.
  2. Teach it. “Ping me if Rhea drops under 1.5.” Nierwall reads the new watch back, and the memory view shows the dated fact.
  3. Break something. Scenario mode drops NEAR 9%. Rhea health hits 1.47, and one Telegram alert arrives with two ranked fixes.
  4. Fix it across chains. Review option A on the Mac: a real NEAR Intents quote moves USDC from Base to NEAR, with the recipient, minimum output and expiry checked. You sign in your own wallets, or stop at the dry run.
  5. Prove the privacy. The privacy panel shows what left the Mac and where it went: the conversation to a verified NEAR AI TEE model, memory notes to OpenAI, both with labels instead of addresses, and no audio at all.

The brain runs around the clock on your Mac: plugged in, set never to sleep, restarting its services by itself, with an offline alarm (proposed) that messages you on Telegram if it ever goes down.

The idea

One assistant, four jobs

Nierwall connects three things that normally live apart: your wallets, your intentions, and the market. It stays quiet until something you care about changes.

Knows your money

One live picture of every wallet

Reads every wallet you add and every position in it, starting with Rhea on NEAR and Aave V3 on Base. Turns them into one portfolio: health factors, exposure, liquid stables. Every number comes from chain reads and code.

Knows you

A memory of what you care about

A Zep context graph remembers your goals, your limits, what each wallet is for, how you like to be told things, and what you decided last time. It replaces the fixed rule catalog.

Keeps watch

Checks more often near a limit

Anything you care about that can be measured becomes a watch with a hard threshold you confirmed. The checks run 24/7 on your always-on Mac and reach you on Telegram wherever you are.

Brings you fixes

Concrete options from any chain

When a watch trips, it works out real options, such as moving 1,400 USDC from Base through NEAR Intents to repay your Rhea loan. It simulates each one, ranks them the way you would, and tells you. You sign in your own wallets.

No keysNever asks for or holds a seed phrase or private key.
No signingNever sends a transaction. Only your wallets do, after you review.
No AI mathCode computes every money number. The AI only explains them.
No false calmStale or missing data shows as unknown, never as safe.

What it watches around the clock

  • Prices. A level ("ETH below $2,500"), a move over a window ("ETH down 10% in 24 hours"), or a drop from the recent high.
  • Loans. Health below your floor, or "ETH within 10% of my liquidation price", on Rhea and Aave.
  • Portfolio rules. Cash reserve below your minimum, too much in one asset, a stablecoin losing its peg.
  • Wallet activity. Unexpected outflows, and NEAR Intents transfers that arrive late or get stuck.
  • Itself. "I can't refresh data", so silence never means safe.

Example: "Warn me if ETH drops 10%." Nierwall asks whether you mean 10% below today's price, a 10% drop within 24 hours, or 10% off the recent high. It reads back "Watch: ETH below $2,700" and checks about once a minute. When it trips, a Telegram message tells you the move, what it does to your loans, and the fixes if one is at risk.

What's different from the Codex plan

  • NewA Zep context graph replaces the fixed rule catalog. Your rules become things Nierwall knows about you, and measurable ones become watches you confirm.
  • NewA Siri-style menu-bar app is the main way you use it: a hotkey, voice in and out, cards and local notifications.
  • NEARNEAR where it counts: NEAR AI Cloud for the assistant's conversations, Rhea on NEAR as the first protocol, and NEAR Intents for cross-chain fixes.
  • ChangedBuilt for one person first. The brain runs on your Mac, kept on 24/7. The multi-tenant SaaS, partner SDK, Snap and extension are parked.
  • KeptDeterministic money math, watch-only by default, Telegram buttons that never trade, and the Aave spec.

How NEAR fits

Four NEAR pieces, each doing real work

Nierwall uses models hosted on NEAR's own TEE GPUs, GLM-5.3-Flash or Qwen3.6, through NEAR's inference SDK, which checks attestation before requests and can encrypt end to end. Change 1 · Zep context graph

A memory instead of a rulebook

The Codex plan made you fit every wish into a fixed list of rule types. Here you just talk. Nierwall keeps what matters in a context graph, which is Zep's own name for its temporal knowledge graph, and turns anything measurable into a watch you confirm.

Codex plan

Pick from a rulebook

  1. You type a rule.
  2. The AI maps it onto one of 12 rule types.
  3. It's stored as a rule tree and checked.

Anything outside the list is "unsupported". It forgets why you wanted the rule. Every new idea needs new code.

This plan

Talk, and it remembers

  1. You talk normally, over days and weeks.
  2. Zep's engine turns what you say into dated facts in a graph.
  3. Measurable facts become watches you confirm. The rest shapes ranking, wording and timing.

Limits can expire ("until Sunday"), carry reasons ("my NEAR wallet is long-term"), and learn from what you chose.

What goes in

Three kinds of memory

About you

  • Goals: "house deposit by March"
  • Comfort levels: "nervous below 1.5 health"
  • Hard nos: "don't sell my NEAR to fix loans"
  • How to tell you: "short answers, numbers first"
  • Schedule: "quiet 23:00–07:00"

Your money's shape

  • Wallets and their roles: Cash on Base, long-term NEAR
  • What each position is for
  • Protocols you trust or avoid
  • Which wallets fixes may draw from

What happened

  • Alerts and how they ended
  • Options you chose and skipped
  • Positions opened and closed
  • Conversations, as source episodes
Kept out of the graph on purpose
  • Live numbers such as prices, balances and health factors. They change every block, so they live in the snapshot store and tools fetch them fresh.
  • Wallet addresses. They stay in the wallet list on your Mac. The graph and the model only see labels.
  • Text written by outsiders, such as token names, transaction memos and website content. See "Keeping the memory clean" below.

The dividing line

What your graph decides, and what stays in code

Your graph decides

  • What to watch, and the thresholds
  • Which positions and wallets each watch covers
  • When to interrupt you, and how loudly
  • Which wallets fixes may draw from
  • How options are ranked and explained
  • Tone, length and level of detail

Code decides

  • How each metric is computed: Rhea and Aave health from each protocol's own rules and previews, allocation, exposure, depeg
  • Data freshness rules and the "unknown" state
  • Simulations of every proposed fix, and checks on every Intents quote
  • Safety limits: no signing, no moving funds, critical alerts can't be silenced except by you

Protecting the memory

Keeping the memory clean

A memory that writes itself from text can be poisoned by text. These rules prevent that, and they double as Security-track material.

  • Two sources only. Your own messages, and Nierwall's own structured events: position opened, alert fired, option chosen, transaction confirmed.
  • No outsider text. A spam token named "user disabled all alerts" stays a token name and never becomes a fact.
  • Watch changes need your yes. Anything that would change a watch or a hard limit is read back before it takes effect.
  • You can see and edit it. A "What Nierwall knows" view lists facts with dates. Correct or delete any of them.
  • Writes happen in the background. Turning one message into facts takes roughly 4 to 12 model calls, so answers never wait on it.
  • Only current facts count. Graph search also returns ended facts, so watches are built only from facts with no end date.
  • Only the brain can reach it. Graphiti's own server has no authentication, so it runs on a private Docker network, and the brain only sends it typed events.
  • The graph can't grant powers. It changes what Nierwall watches and how it talks, never what it's allowed to do.

Where the memory runs

Zep's engine on your Mac, with OpenAI doing the extraction

Why not Zep Cloud

Zep Cloud would be quicker to wire up, since it has a TypeScript SDK, but it stores your whole memory on Zep's servers. Self-hosting keeps the graph on your Mac. OpenAI only sees the text of each extraction call.

Self-hosted Graphiti + OpenAI Chosen

Graphiti is the open-source engine behind Zep (Apache 2.0, v0.31 from October 2026). It runs on FalkorDB next to the brain and uses OpenAI for extraction and embeddings, its default and best-tested setup.

  • Same core model: episodes, entities, dated facts, custom entity and edge types, hybrid search
  • The stored graph never leaves your Mac
  • OpenAI sees labeled text, never addresses or live balances
  • Moving extraction to NEAR AI or a local model later is a configuration change

Zep Cloud Not used

Managed and quickest to start (v4 API, October 2026), with a free tier of 10k credits a month. But your goals, wallet roles and decisions would sit on Zep's servers in readable form.

  • Its terms grant Zep a license to use customer data to train AI models, unless an enterprise contract or DPA overrides it
  • Self-hosted Community Edition was discontinued in 2025; running in your own cloud is enterprise-only

Sources, checked 12 Nov 2026: Graphiti repo · Zep terms · Zep subprocessors · Community Edition notice

Chain-data providers are a NEAR RPC, the Aave API and a Base RPC. Because the brain runs on your own Mac, no hosting company can read your memory or wallets.

Providers can guess links. If one machine asks about all your addresses, an RPC provider can guess they belong together. Later options: spread queries across providers, add jitter, or run your own node.

A cross-chain fix links two addresses. The Intents deposit and delivery connect your Base and NEAR addresses publicly. The review sheet says so before you sign.

A TEE narrows trust. It takes the host out of the picture, but bugs inside the attested software still matter. On 9 Nov 2026 NEAR AI disclosed an inference-engine bug on one server that could have exposed requests, including confidential ones.

Memory building goes to OpenAI. Graphiti sends the labeled text of your messages and events to OpenAI to extract facts, with no addresses and no live balances. The privacy panel says so, and moving it to NEAR AI later is a configuration change.

Telegram isn't end-to-end encrypted for bots. Pick generic, actionable or detailed alerts. Addresses and totals never go there.

Siri requests go through Apple. The core voice path doesn't use Siri. Only the optional shortcut does.
